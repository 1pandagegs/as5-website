What Milestone Governance Actually Prevents

A missed milestone is rarely the real problem — it's the first visible sign of one that started weeks earlier.

Milestone governance sounds like paperwork. In practice, it's the single mechanism most responsible for catching problems while they're still cheap to fix.

A missed milestone is rarely the actual problem. It's a symptom — evidence that something upstream, a supplier delay, a design clash, an underestimated scope, started going wrong weeks earlier and simply wasn't visible until the deadline arrived.

Without structured milestone sign-off, that lag is invisible by design: everyone assumes things are on track because no one has explicitly confirmed otherwise. With it, every phase requires an explicit, documented confirmation before the next one begins — which means problems surface at the point they're introduced, not the point they become expensive.

At AS5, no phase begins until the previous one is verified, not just reported as complete. That single rule eliminates the most common failure mode in construction: work continuing on top of an unresolved problem because stopping to check felt like it would cost more time than it saved.

"The cost of pausing to verify is always smaller than the cost of discovering the same problem three phases later."
